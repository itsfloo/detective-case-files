← Back to all cases Easy Death at Rosewood Mansion Case file A locked study, a cold cup of tea, and three people who all say they were somewhere else. Scenario Lord Alden, the owner of Rosewood Mansion, was found dead in his study at 9 PM. The door was locked from the inside, and his own key was still in his pocket. There were no signs of a struggle. Only three people were in the house that evening: his niece, his butler, and his family doctor. Beside the body stood an empty teacup. Your job is to work out who did it, how, and why. Suspects Clara Niece Personality: Polite, nervous, avoids eye contact. Alibi: Says she was reading in the library all evening. Mr. Hale Butler Personality: Formal, loyal, talks a lot. Alibi: Says he was in the kitchen all evening. Dr. Voss Family doctor Personality: Cold, impatient, defensive. Alibi: Says he left the house at 8 PM. Clues c1Torn will draft Found on the study desk. A new will that removes one family member, torn in half. c2Empty teacup Found in the study. A bitter residue is left at the bottom. c3Kitchen key Found in the hallway outside the study. A small letter “C” is scratched into it. c4Overdue letter Found in Dr. Voss’s coat. It asks him to repay a large sum he owes Lord Alden. c5Butler’s evening log Found in the kitchen. It says the tea tray was collected at 8:40 PM by Miss Clara. c6Library book Found in the library. The bookmark is still on page one, and the chair is covered in dust. Questions to think about 1 Who had the opportunity to reach the tea? 2 How could the victim die without any sign of a struggle? 3 Who gains the most from the torn document, and who only looks guilty? Solution Reveal the solution Culprit: Clara. Motive: Lord Alden had drafted a new will that cut her out. She learned about it and could not accept losing her inheritance. Method: She prepared the evening tea herself and poisoned it. Lord Alden drank it alone in his study, which is why the locked door never mattered. Proof: The torn will draft (c1) gives her the motive. The butler’s log (c5) shows she collected the tea tray. Her kitchen key (c3) shows she had access to the kitchen. The untouched library book (c6) breaks her alibi. Red herring: Dr. Voss owed money (c4), but nothing connects him to the tea. Endings Correct culprit, good reasoning. You name Clara and explain the will, the tea tray, and the broken alibi. She confesses, and the case is closed. Correct culprit, weak reasoning. You name Clara, but you cannot explain how. She denies everything, and the police need more time to find the proof. Wrong culprit. You accuse someone else. Clara slips away, and the case goes cold. Case Files · Back to all cases
