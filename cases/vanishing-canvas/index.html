Medium

The Vanishing Canvas

Case file

A bare frame, a quiet alarm, and three staff members who all say they were somewhere else.

Scenario

The Larkspur Town Museum is small, and its pride is a painting called Harbor at Dawn. On Friday, the museum insured it for a large sum ahead of a planned sale to a private collector, which would have taken it out of public view for good.

On Saturday at 7 AM, the night guard made his last round and found the east gallery silent. The frame was still on the wall, but the painting was gone. The gallery door was locked, and there was no broken glass and no sign of a forced entry.

Only three staff members had access to the building that week: the head curator, the night guard, and the painting restorer. Your job is to work out who took it, how, and why.

Suspects

Odette Marsh

Head curator

Personality: Precise, proud, easily flustered.

Alibi: Says she left at 6 PM and spent the evening at a restaurant across town.

Felix Brandt

Night guard

Personality: Easygoing, chatty, a little lazy.

Alibi: Says he did his rounds at 7 PM and 9 PM, and spent the rest of the night in the break room.

Juno Reyes

Painting restorer

Personality: Quiet, perfectionist, dry sense of humor.

Alibi: Says she went home at 5 PM and spent the evening alone.

Clues

c1Alarm log

Found on the security panel. The east gallery alarm was switched off at 7:50 PM with the head curator’s code and switched back on at 8:05 PM.

c2Row of screws

Found on the east gallery floor. The back panel of the frame was unscrewed, and the screws were lined up neatly in a row.

c3Stained glove

Found in the loading bay trash can. A single white cotton glove with a sticky patch of varnish, the kind used only in the restoration workshop.

c4Restaurant receipt

Found in the head curator’s coat. It is stamped 7:45 PM, from a restaurant twenty minutes away by car.

c5Guard’s notebook

Found in the guard’s office. One entry reads: “7:55 PM, heard the loading bay door. Probably a delivery. Back to the match.”

c6Final notice

Found in the guard’s locker. A betting shop demands that he repay a large debt by the end of the month.

c7Empty tube rack

Found in the restoration workshop. One long cardboard shipping tube is missing, and the roll of acid-free tissue paper beside it is half used.

c8Sticky note

Found in the workshop’s shared supply drawer. It holds the curator’s alarm code in her handwriting, with the words “in case I forget.”

Questions to think about

  1. Who could have switched off the alarm at 7:50 PM, and who could not?
  2. How could someone take a painting from a locked gallery without breaking anything?
  3. Why would a thief take a painting that is far too famous to sell?

Solution

Reveal the solution

Culprit: Juno Reyes.

Motive: She spent two years restoring Harbor at Dawn and believed it belonged to the town. When she learned it would be sold to a private collector, she decided to take it and keep it from disappearing into a private home.

Method: She copied the curator’s alarm code from the sticky note in the workshop drawer and disarmed the east gallery at 7:50 PM. She unscrewed the back panel carefully, as someone used to handling frames would, rolled the canvas into a shipping tube, and left through the loading bay. She re-armed the alarm at 8:05 PM.

Proof: The alarm log (c1) shows the curator’s code was used, but her receipt (c4) places her across town, so someone else knew the code. The sticky note (c8) shows the code sat in the workshop drawer. The neat row of screws (c2) points to a trained hand. The varnish-stained glove (c3) and the missing tube (c7) both come from the workshop. The guard’s notebook (c5) confirms someone used the loading bay in that time window. Together these break her alibi.

Red herrings: Mr. Brandt owed money (c6) and had keys to everything, but nothing ties him to the workshop or the code. Ms. Marsh had a motive in the insurance payout, but her receipt (c4) clears her.

Endings

Correct culprit, good reasoning. You name Juno and explain the alarm code, the workshop glove, and the missing tube. She sighs, admits she hid the painting in her attic, and returns it. The sale is cancelled, and the town votes to keep Harbor at Dawn on public display.
Correct culprit, weak reasoning. You name Juno, but you cannot explain how she got past the alarm. She calmly denies everything, and the police need more time to find the painting.
Wrong culprit. You accuse someone else. Juno quietly returns to her workshop, and Harbor at Dawn is never seen again.
